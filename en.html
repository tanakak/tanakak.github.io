Research

Understanding decisions and designing policies in a climate-risk society. The Tanaka Laboratory studies how individuals and communities perceive, communicate, and respond to climate and disaster risks. Grounded in environmental economics, we combine survey research, choice experiments, factorial survey experiments, spatial data analysis, and machine learning to evaluate behavioral responses, public preferences, and policy outcomes.

Disaster risk communication & evacuation

Clarity and credibility of disaster information, trust in warnings, false-alarm effects, information fatigue, and evacuation decisions.

Climate adaptation & urban resilience

Preparedness for floods, earthquakes, and extreme heat, and pathways toward sustainable, resilient communities.

Green infrastructure & environmental policy

Benefits such as risk reduction, heat mitigation, and ecosystem conservation, and policies acceptable to citizens and organizations.

Sustainable mobility & urban space

Walking, cycling, public transport, traffic calming, and street redesign for healthier and more sustainable cities.

Across these areas, we seek to develop an integrated science of decision-making for climate-risk societies. We welcome undergraduate and graduate students interested in applying empirical methods to real-world environmental and societal challenges.
